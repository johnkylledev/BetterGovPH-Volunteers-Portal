# Volunteer Onboarding: Requirements (What and Why)

Status: proposed. Technical approach: see [volunteer-onboarding-implementation.md](volunteer-onboarding-implementation.md).

## 1. Background
BetterGov.ph exists to make Philippine government information and services more accessible, transparent and usable, through open-source work done by volunteers. Our volunteer opportunities already include developers, data engineers, researchers, designers and community amplifiers, and our open-government fellowship work covers datasets, APIs, budget transparency, government service delivery and civic accountability. Our contribution model welcomes code, ideas, bug reports, translations, documentation and data collection.

Our volunteers are not only programmers. They are also researchers, designers, writers, data checkers, translators, community organizers, lawyers, trainers and more.

Today our sign-up asks everyone to describe themselves as a developer. A researcher or community organizer has to pick a tech role and list tech skills just to join. That turns away people we want, and it gives us inaccurate information about who our volunteers really are.

## 2. Goal
Anyone who wants to help can join and describe how they can contribute, whether or not they write code. "Everyone can contribute, even if they don't code."

## 3. Objectives
1. **Welcome non-developers.** A researcher, writer or organizer can finish sign-up without pretending to be a developer.
2. **Know our volunteers better.** Capture what kind of work people do, what they are good at, which public issues they care about, how they want to help, and how much time they have.
3. **Make matching possible.** When a project or volunteer call needs, say, a budget analyst interested in public spending, we can find the right people instead of guessing.
4. **Keep sign-up short.** Required steps stay few; extra details are optional and can be added later.
5. **Don't disrupt existing members.** Current volunteers keep their access and records, and can enrich their profiles whenever they like.
6. **Stay mission-specific.** Use language that reflects civic tech and open government, not a generic volunteer list.

## 5. What we want to capture

### 5.1 Type of work (role family)
Each volunteer picks one main area, then (where it applies) a more specific role.

| # | Role family | Example roles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Software Engineering | Frontend, backend, full-stack, mobile, QA, DevOps |
| 2 | Data & AI | Data engineer, data scientist, AI/ML developer, data annotator |
| 3 | Design & User Experience | UI/UX designer, product designer, accessibility specialist, user researcher |
| 4 | Research & Public Policy | Policy researcher, legislative researcher, budget analyst, fact-checker |
| 5 | Open Data & Transparency | Data collector, open data analyst, procurement monitor, data verifier |
| 6 | Product & Project Management | Product manager, project manager, business analyst, scrum facilitator |
| 7 | Content & Communications | Writer, editor, translator, journalist, social media creator |
| 8 | Community & Advocacy | Community organizer, digital advocate, volunteer coordinator, partnership liaison |
| 9 | Digital Public Services | Service designer, citizen support, government service researcher, usability tester |
| 10 | Legal, Governance & Compliance | Legal researcher, privacy specialist, governance advisor |
| 11 | Education & Digital Literacy | Trainer, mentor, workshop facilitator, digital literacy advocate |
| 12 | Operations & Partnerships | Events organizer, administrative support, fundraising, institutional partnerships |

Today's developer roles (Frontend, Backend, Full Stack, UI/UX, DevOps, Data & AI, Security, Project Manager) stay and sit inside these areas.

### 5.2 Skills (and how strong)
Volunteers pick several skills, independent of their main role, from a short curated list of roughly 60 to 80. Each skill is rated Learner, Practitioner or Expert. Starting point, grouped:

- **Software Engineering:** React / Next.js, JavaScript / TypeScript, Python, Node.js, APIs, Databases / SQL, Git / GitHub, Testing / QA, DevOps / Cloud, Cybersecurity
- **Data & AI:** Data Collection, Web Scraping, Data Cleaning, ETL / Data Pipelines, Data Analysis, Data Visualization, GIS / Mapping, Machine Learning, Generative AI / LLMs, Data Annotation
- **Design & User Experience:** UI Design, UX Research, Figma, Prototyping, Design Systems, Accessibility / WCAG, Service Design, Information Architecture
- **Research & Governance:** Public Policy Research, Legislative Research, Budget Analysis, Procurement Analysis, Fact-checking, Freedom of Information, Data Verification, Monitoring & Evaluation
- **Communications & Community:** Content Writing, Editing, Translation, Graphic Design, Video Production, Social Media, Community Management, Public Speaking, Advocacy Campaigns
- **Management & Professional:** Product Management, Project Management, Business Analysis, Volunteer Coordination, Legal Research, Data Privacy, Fundraising, Partnership Development, Training & Mentorship

Volunteers can also add their own skill if it isn't listed. The final wording of the list is an open question (see section 12).

Separate from role and skills: a developer may want to work only on education transparency. Twelve causes, aligned with our public-interest projects and fellowship challenge areas:

| Cause | What it covers |

| Open Data | Public datasets, FOI, data access |
| Public Spending | Budgets, procurement, government expenditure |
| Democracy & Elections | Electoral information, political accountability |
| Digital Government | Public services, citizen experience |
| Education | Schools, learning outcomes, digital literacy |
| Healthcare | Public health, health information access |
| Infrastructure | Public works, projects, monitoring |
| Environment & Energy | Climate, sustainability, energy transparency |
| Justice & Human Rights | Access to justice, rights awareness |
| Local Communities | LGU services, citizen participation |
| Digital Inclusion | Accessibility, underserved communities |

### 5.4 How they want to help (contribution types)
Different from role: this is the style of help. A person can pick more than one.

| Type | What it means |
|---|---|
| Builder | Develops software, digital products, datasets or tools |
| Researcher | Investigates public issues, policies and data |
| Contributor | Performs defined tasks such as data verification, translation or testing |
| Advocate | Promotes civic participation, transparency and awareness |
| Organizer | Coordinates people, events and community activities |
| Mentor | Shares expertise and gives professional guidance |
| Supporter | Provides resources, sponsorship, infrastructure or professional services |

### 5.5 Experience and time
- **Experience level:** Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Professional/Expert.
- **Weekly availability:** 1 to 2, 3 to 5, 5 to 10, or 10+ hours a week.

### 5.6 Optional extras
Never required, can be added at sign-up or later:
- Location and languages spoken.
- Links to social profiles: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, GitHub and Discord, plus a portfolio or website.
- A self-reported GitHub link here is only a profile link. Verified GitHub linking is a separate piece of work.

## 6. The experience we want
- Sign-up is a few short steps: who you are, how you want to help, your skills and interests, then connecting Discord.
- People see themselves in the choices. Non-coding roles are shown clearly as valid ("No coding required").
- Developers still get the extra checks that help us confirm tech skills; other roles are asked only for what makes sense for them.
- Choosing a cause is easy and motivating (tappable choices with a one-line description), not a form field.
- Optional details never block anyone from finishing, and can be skipped and filled later.
- Progress is not lost: someone who leaves half-way can come back to what they entered.
- Existing members see a gentle reminder to complete their profile, not a forced redo, and can edit their profile from their dashboard.
- Messages are friendly and say plainly what is missing.

## 7. What admins and the team can do with it
- Filter and export volunteers by work area, skill, cause, availability and how they want to help.
- See a volunteer's fuller profile when reviewing an application.
- Find people for a project or volunteer call (manually at first) instead of asking in the general channel.
- Keep the public member verification page unchanged: it shows only what it shows today plus, at most, the work area. Links and availability stay private.

## 8. Requirements summary
Must have
- A non-developer can complete sign-up end to end.
- The 12 work areas, curated skills with levels, 12 causes, 7 ways to help, experience and weekly availability can be recorded.
- Developers keep today's skill checks; no other area is blocked by them.
- Existing members keep access and data.
- Optional extras (location, languages, social links) are available but not required.

Should have
- Existing members can enrich their profile later from their dashboard.
- Admins can filter and export by the new information.
- Public pages (Landing, Contribute) describe the 12 areas and the "Everyone can contribute" message.

Could have (later)
- Mapping to international standards.
- Contribution records and automatic matching.

## 9. Why this approach
- It reflects BetterGov's actual work (datasets, budgets, public services, accountability), not a generic volunteer list.
- Work type, skills, causes and style of help are separate, because they vary independently. A developer may care most about education; a lawyer may want to mentor.
- It is deliberately simpler than international standards, but is designed so it can be mapped to them later. The reference standards are: ESCO for skills, ISCO-08 or ESCO occupations for roles, the UN Volunteers typology for volunteering concepts, and the UN Sustainable Development Goals (adapted) for civic causes. Our categories are an application-level vocabulary, not an official international classification.
- Our simple levels (Learner, Practitioner, Expert) are a custom scale, easy for volunteers to understand.
- The long-term picture is Volunteer, then Roles, Skills, Causes, Projects and Contributions, so we can eventually see not only who registered but who fixed bugs, verified datasets, wrote documentation, did research and mentored others. This work builds the first half of that picture.
- Sizing (12 areas, about 60 to 80 skills, 12 causes, 7 ways to help) gives enough detail for matching without an overwhelming sign-up.

## 10. Tradeoffs we accept
| Choice | What we gain | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| 12 work areas | Clearer self-identification and better matching | More choice at sign-up; some areas overlap (for example Research, Open Data, Digital Public Services) |
| Keep today's developer roles and add new areas on top | Nothing existing breaks | Two lists to maintain for a while |
| Developer-only skill checks | Keeps quality signal for tech, unblocks others | Non-developer skills are taken on trust; rules differ by area |
| Self-reported skill levels | Quick, low friction | Cannot be verified automatically; admins remain the quality check |
| Optional social links | Richer profile and outreach options | Privacy care required; fewer people will fill them |
| Self-reported GitHub link now | Works for anyone immediately | May differ from a verified GitHub account later |
| No new required fields for existing members | No disruption | Slower fill rate of the new information |
| Profile only, not contributions | Smaller, safer first step | Matching and impact tracking come later |

## 11. Privacy and trust
- Social links, location and availability are visible only to the member and BetterGov admins, not on the public verification page.
- Everything beyond the basics is optional, and the sign-up should say why we ask.
- Volunteers can edit or remove optional details later.
- Skill levels and links are self-reported; we do not claim they are verified.

## 12. Success looks like
- Non-developers can sign up and finish without help.
- A growing share of members have a work area, skills, causes and availability filled in.
- Admins can filter and export volunteers by area, skill and cause.
- No existing member loses access or is forced back through sign-up.
- Fewer people abandon sign-up at the role and skills steps.

## 13. Out of scope (for now)
- Recording individual contributions and impact.
- Automatic matching of volunteers to projects.
- Verifying social or GitHub accounts (covered by the GitHub account linking plan).
- Formal mapping to ESCO/ISCO or other standards.

## 14. Open questions
- Final wording and count of the curated skills list.
- Whether 12 work areas should be merged or reordered after early feedback.
- Confirm data and privacy handling for social links with the Supabase/admin owners before launch.
- Whether availability and "how you want to help" should be visible to project leads, not only admins.
